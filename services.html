Most Common Use

Treatment-Resistant Depression

Treatment-resistant depression is typically defined as failing to respond adequately to at least two different antidepressant medications at therapeutic doses. It affects roughly 30% of people with major depressive disorder.

⚡

Rapid Onset — Often Within 24 Hours

Standard antidepressants take 4–8 weeks to show effect. Ketamine often produces measurable improvement within 24 hours of the first infusion — critical for patients in acute crisis.

🔬

Different Mechanism

SSRIs and SNRIs work on serotonin and norepinephrine. Ketamine works on NMDA glutamate receptors — a completely different pathway. That's why it works when everything else has failed.

📊

~70% Response Rate in Resistant Cases

Multiple controlled studies show ketamine produces a significant antidepressant response in approximately 70% of treatment-resistant patients — far higher than adding another antidepressant.

🛡️

Reduces Suicidal Ideation

Ketamine is one of the few interventions shown to rapidly reduce suicidal thinking — a critical benefit when waiting weeks for medication to work is not an option.

Trauma-Related

PTSD

Post-traumatic stress disorder affects an estimated 20 million Americans. First-line treatments — SSRIs and trauma-focused therapy — leave roughly 40% of patients without adequate relief.

🧠

Reduces Emotional Charge of Memories

Ketamine may work for PTSD by disrupting the reconsolidation of traumatic memories — reducing their emotional weight without erasing them.

📉

Symptom Reduction in Clinical Trials

Studies at NYU and other research centers show significant reductions in PTSD symptom severity after a series of ketamine infusions versus placebo.

🤝

Best Combined with Therapy

Ketamine creates a window of neuroplasticity. Many patients find trauma-focused therapy significantly more effective during and shortly after an infusion series.

Pain Conditions

Chronic Pain & CRPS

Ketamine was originally developed as an anesthetic. Its pain-blocking properties are well established — and it works for chronic pain conditions through a mechanism called "central sensitization reset."

Complex Regional Pain Syndrome (CRPS)

CRPS is one of the most painful conditions known to medicine. Ketamine infusions have the strongest evidence base for CRPS of any of the pain conditions listed here.

Fibromyalgia

Fibromyalgia involves central sensitization — the nervous system amplifying pain signals. Ketamine interrupts this cycle, often producing weeks to months of reduced pain.

Neuropathic Pain

Nerve damage pain from diabetes, chemotherapy, or injury that hasn't responded to standard neuropathic medications may respond to ketamine's NMDA-blocking effect.

Migraine

Intractable migraine — chronic migraine unresponsive to standard treatments — is one of the emerging areas in ketamine pain therapy.

What It Costs

Ketamine Infusion Pricing in Huntsville

Ketamine therapy is not covered by most insurance plans. Here's what to expect in terms of cost.

💉

Initial Series (6 Infusions)

A standard series of 6 infusions typically costs $2,400–$3,600 total, or $400–$600 per infusion. This is the most common starting protocol for depression and PTSD.

🔄

Maintenance Infusions

After a successful initial series, some patients need booster infusions every 4–8 weeks. Single maintenance infusions typically run $400–$600 each.

💊

Chronic Pain Protocols

Pain protocols may require higher doses or longer infusion times. Pain series typically run $500–$800 per infusion depending on duration and dosing.

📋

Consultation

Initial medical consultation is required before any infusion. Consultation fee varies — ask when scheduling. Some clinics apply it toward your first infusion.

Insurance: Ketamine infusion therapy for psychiatric and pain conditions is generally not covered by insurance. Some patients use HSA/FSA funds. We do not accept insurance assignment but can provide documentation for possible reimbursement claims.
