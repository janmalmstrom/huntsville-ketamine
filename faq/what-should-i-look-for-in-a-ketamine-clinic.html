What should I look for in a ketamine clinic?

Quick Answer: Look for physician-administered IV infusions, evidence-based dosing protocols, transparent response-rate statistics, and willingness to coordinate with your existing psychiatric or pain care team.

Key Takeaways

  • Physician oversight matters: infusions should be administered or directly supervised by a board-certified MD, not delegated entirely to nursing staff.
  • Verify the clinic uses evidence-based dosing — the Berman et al. NIMH-validated protocol (0.5 mg/kg IV over 40 minutes) is the clinical standard for treatment-resistant depression.
  • Roughly 70% of treatment-resistant depression patients respond to IV ketamine in published trials (NEJM, JAMA Psychiatry) — any clinic claiming higher should be asked to cite the data.
  • A legitimate clinic will coordinate with your prescribing psychiatrist or pain specialist, not operate in isolation from your existing care.
  • Avoid clinics that cannot clearly explain their screening process, monitoring equipment, or how they handle adverse events during infusion.

Why the Clinic's Medical Structure Is the First Thing to Verify

Ketamine produces cardiovascular and dissociative effects during infusion. Blood pressure can rise significantly. Heart rate fluctuates. Some patients experience brief perceptual disturbances. These are manageable — but only if a qualified physician is present and monitoring, not reviewing charts from another room.

Ask directly: Is a board-certified physician present in the suite during every infusion? What specialty — anesthesiology, psychiatry, internal medicine? What monitoring equipment is used? If answers are vague, that is a signal worth taking seriously.

Nurse-only clinics exist. Some operate legally under physician supervision. But "supervision" can mean different things. You want physician presence, not physician availability by phone.

What an Evidence-Based Dosing Protocol Actually Looks Like

The most replicated dosing protocol in the published literature — established through NIMH-funded research by Carlos Zarate Jr. and foundational work by Berman et al. — uses 0.5 mg/kg of IV ketamine administered over 40 minutes. Most treatment-resistant depression patients receive six infusions over two to three weeks.

This specific dose and duration emerged from controlled trials, not clinical intuition. Deviations are not automatically wrong — chronic pain protocols differ — but the clinic should be able to explain why their protocol varies and cite the evidence behind it.

Esketamine (Spravato), the FDA-approved nasal spray, is a related compound. It is not the same as IV racemic ketamine. Response rates, bioavailability, and the clinical experience differ. A transparent clinic will explain this distinction rather than treating the two interchangeably.

💡 Clinical note: The standard six-infusion induction series for depression is derived from the protocols used in NIMH-funded trials. Ask any prospective clinic how their induction series compares — and what their data shows on response rates in their own patient population.

Honest Response Rates: What the Research Says and What Clinics Should Tell You

Published trials report approximately 70% of treatment-resistant depression patients show meaningful response to IV ketamine. That figure comes from controlled research published in NEJM and JAMA Psychiatry. It is not a guarantee — it means roughly 30% do not respond.

A clinic worth using will tell you both numbers without being asked. If a clinic's marketing presents ketamine as universally effective, that is a red flag. Treatment-resistant means these are patients who have already failed SSRIs, SNRIs, lithium augmentation, lamotrigine, or combinations thereof. Honesty about the 30% non-responder rate is a sign of clinical credibility.

Ask what happens if you don't respond after the initial series. Do they refer back to your psychiatrist? Discuss esketamine? Evaluate for ECT? The answer reveals whether they are managing your care or just administering infusions.

⚠️ Important: Ketamine is a treatment, not a cure. Effects in depression and PTSD typically last weeks to months. Most patients require maintenance infusions. Any clinic that implies a single course produces permanent remission is overstating the published evidence.

Screening, Contraindications, and What a Real Intake Process Looks Like

Legitimate clinics screen for contraindications before scheduling your first infusion. Active or history of psychosis, uncontrolled hypertension, certain cardiac conditions, active substance use disorder, and untreated thyroid disease are among the factors that require careful evaluation.

You should complete a medical and psychiatric history intake. A provider should review your medication list — benzodiazepines and lamotrigine can blunt ketamine's effect; naltrexone has significant interactions. If a clinic books you without a thorough intake, the screening is inadequate.

For patients in the Huntsville area — including those at Redstone Arsenal, UAH, HudsonAlpha, or Cummings Research Park — who may have occupational or security-clearance considerations, ask specifically how the clinic handles documentation and what they report. A good clinic answers this question directly.

Clinic Evaluation Checklist

  • Board-certified physician present during infusions (confirm specialty)
  • Evidence-based dosing protocol — can cite Berman et al. or NIMH-validated parameters
  • Transparent response-rate statistics, including non-responder rates
  • Formal medical and psychiatric intake screening with medication review
  • Willingness to coordinate with your existing psychiatrist, therapist, or pain specialist
  • Clear policy on adverse events and emergency response during infusion
  • Honest discussion of maintenance therapy, duration of effect, and what happens if you don't respond
  • Listed with or verifiable through the American Society of Ketamine Physicians certified provider directory

Bottom Line

The most important factors in choosing a ketamine clinic are physician presence during infusions, verifiable evidence-based dosing, honest disclosure of response rates (including the ~30% who don't respond), and a formal intake process that screens for contraindications and reviews your medication list. Ketamine is a serious medical intervention with meaningful evidence behind it — but that evidence depends on it being administered correctly. A clinic that can't clearly answer questions about its protocol, credentials, and non-responder rate is not meeting the standard the research was built on.

Frequently Asked Questions

What credentials should a ketamine clinic physician have in Huntsville, AL?

Look for board certification in anesthesiology, psychiatry, or internal medicine — these specialties have the most direct training relevant to ketamine's cardiovascular and psychiatric effects. The physician should be present during infusions, not simply listed as medical director. Confirm their credentials are verifiable through the Alabama Board of Medical Examiners.

How do I know if a ketamine clinic is using the right dosing protocol?

The standard for treatment-resistant depression, established through NIMH-funded research and Berman et al., is 0.5 mg/kg IV over 40 minutes, typically six infusions over two to three weeks. Ask the clinic to explain their protocol and what published evidence supports any deviations. Clinics that can't cite a specific protocol source should be questioned further.

What's the difference between IV ketamine and Spravato (esketamine) at a clinic?

Spravato is FDA-approved intranasal esketamine, a related but distinct compound with lower and more variable bioavailability than IV ketamine. IV racemic ketamine has a larger published evidence base for treatment-resistant depression, while Spravato is administered in a certified healthcare setting with mandatory two-hour observation. A qualified clinic will explain which is appropriate for your diagnosis and history rather than defaulting to one option for all patients.

Does a ketamine clinic in Huntsville need to coordinate with my psychiatrist?

Coordination with your existing psychiatrist or pain specialist is a marker of clinical quality, not optional. Ketamine's antidepressant effects are typically temporary — often weeks to months — and integrating it with your ongoing medication management (SSRIs, lithium, lamotrigine) improves long-term outcomes. A clinic that operates without contacting your prescribing provider is missing a critical component of responsible care.

What screening should a legitimate ketamine clinic do before my first infusion?

A thorough intake includes medical history, psychiatric history, current medications, cardiovascular assessment, and screening for contraindications including history of psychosis, uncontrolled hypertension, and active substance use disorder. Your medication list should be reviewed carefully — benzodiazepines and lamotrigine can reduce ketamine's efficacy, and naltrexone creates significant pharmacological interactions. If a clinic books you without this level of intake, that is a red flag.

Is a 70% response rate realistic, or is that marketing?

The approximately 70% response rate in treatment-resistant depression comes from controlled clinical trials published in peer-reviewed journals including NEJM and JAMA Psychiatry, not from individual clinic marketing materials. It refers to patients who have already failed two or more antidepressants — SSRIs, SNRIs, or augmentation strategies. The honest corollary is that roughly 30% of patients do not respond; any clinic that omits this number is not accurately representing the research.

What should I ask about maintenance therapy when evaluating a Huntsville ketamine clinic?

Ketamine's antidepressant effects in most patients last weeks to months, not indefinitely. Ask the clinic what their maintenance protocol looks like — how they determine when a booster infusion is needed, what the typical interval is, and what the cost structure is beyond the initial series. A transparent clinic will give you specific timeframes rather than vague reassurances, and will have a plan for patients who do not sustain response.

Searching this directory is a useful starting point for verifying a clinic's standing in the field.
