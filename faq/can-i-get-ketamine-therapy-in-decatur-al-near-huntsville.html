What the Evidence Says About IV Ketamine for Treatment-Resistant Conditions

IV ketamine acts through a fundamentally different mechanism than SSRIs, SNRIs, or lithium. It targets NMDA glutamate receptors instead of the serotonin system. This distinction matters: patients who have failed multiple rounds of conventional antidepressants remain candidates for response to ketamine.

According to NIMH research and randomized controlled trials published in JAMA Psychiatry, approximately 70% of patients with treatment-resistant depression show clinically significant response to IV ketamine. Measurable improvement typically appears within 24 hours of the first infusion — substantially faster than the 4 to 8 weeks required for most antidepressants. For PTSD, the Feder et al. randomized controlled trial (2014, JAMA Psychiatry) documented significant symptom reduction within 24 hours compared to placebo.

Ketamine is not a cure. Approximately 30% of treatment-resistant patients do not respond. Additionally, symptom improvement is time-limited without maintenance therapy — most responding patients experience symptom return within weeks to months without a structured follow-up plan.

Who Is a Candidate? What Decatur Patients Should Know Before Calling

Ideal candidates have documented treatment-resistant major depression, PTSD, bipolar depression, or chronic pain conditions such as complex regional pain syndrome (CRPS) or fibromyalgia. "Treatment-resistant" typically means having received at least 2 antidepressants (SSRIs or SNRIs) at therapeutic doses for adequate duration, or equivalent trials for pain conditions.

Esketamine (Spravato), the FDA-approved nasal spray, is an alternative sometimes covered by insurance. However, IV ketamine offers faster onset and more flexible dosing protocols, and remains the clinical standard across most research trials, including the Berman et al. protocol implemented at established clinics. Patients currently taking benzodiazepines or naltrexone may require medication adjustments before starting — the provider conducts a comprehensive medical intake to identify these considerations.

⚠️ Important: Ketamine is contraindicated in uncontrolled hypertension, active psychosis, and certain cardiovascular conditions. A thorough medical history review occurs before any infusion is scheduled.

Cost and Insurance Reality for Alabama Patients

Most commercial insurance plans available through Tennessee Valley employers — including those serving the Decatur manufacturing sector and employees at Redstone Arsenal or Cummings Research Park — similarly do not cover IV ketamine as a benefit.
