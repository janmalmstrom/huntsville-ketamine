How long does a ketamine infusion take?

By Jennifer Cole

Quick Answer: A single IV ketamine infusion for depression or PTSD typically takes 40–60 minutes, with total clinic time of 2–2.5 hours when you include preparation, monitoring, and recovery.

Key Takeaways

  • The active infusion runs 40–60 minutes; total clinic time is approximately 2–2.5 hours per session due to pre-infusion preparation and mandatory post-infusion monitoring.
  • A standard initial treatment course is 6 infusions delivered over 2–3 weeks, based on the Berman et al. protocol and NIMH-validated dosing standards.
  • You cannot drive after an infusion — you must arrange a ride home; most Huntsville patients plan to take a half-day from work.
  • Response in treatment-resistant depression typically emerges within 24 hours of the first or second infusion, not weeks — a key difference from SSRIs and SNRIs.
  • Roughly 70% of treatment-resistant depression patients respond to a full 6-session course; approximately 30% do not, and honest providers will tell you this upfront.

How Long Is the Infusion Itself?

The IV infusion runs 40–60 minutes for psychiatric indications — depression, PTSD, anxiety, and OCD. Chronic pain protocols, particularly for CRPS, often run longer: 2–4 hours per session at higher cumulative doses. The duration is not arbitrary. It reflects the dosing schedule validated in clinical research, including the landmark Berman et al. study and subsequent NIMH trials that established the 0.5 mg/kg weight-based dosing standard.

A shorter infusion is not a faster path to results. Compressing the timeline alters the pharmacokinetics and has not been shown to produce equivalent outcomes. Clinics offering 20-minute "express" infusions are departing from the published evidence base.

💡 Clinical note: Chronic pain patients, including those with CRPS or fibromyalgia, typically receive longer infusions at different dosing intervals than psychiatric patients. If you're being treated for pain rather than depression or PTSD, ask your provider specifically how your protocol differs from the psychiatric standard.

What Happens Before and After the Infusion?

The active infusion is only part of your appointment. Plan for a total of 2–2.5 hours at the clinic, broken down as follows:

  • Pre-infusion (20–30 minutes): Vital sign check, IV placement, medication review, and a brief assessment by the supervising physician.
  • Active infusion (40–60 minutes): Continuous monitoring of blood pressure, heart rate, and oxygen saturation throughout.
  • Recovery (30–45 minutes): You remain monitored until dissociative effects have fully resolved and vitals are stable.

You will not be discharged until the clinical team confirms you are alert and oriented. You cannot drive yourself home — this is a safety requirement, not a suggestion. Arrange a ride before every session. Patients working at Redstone Arsenal, UAH, or Cummings Research Park typically find it practical to schedule on a half-day off rather than planning to return to detailed technical work afterward.

How Many Sessions and Over What Timeframe?

A standard initial course for treatment-resistant depression is 6 infusions over 2–3 weeks — typically three sessions per week. This schedule derives from the Berman et al. protocol, the dosing framework cited in NIMH research and replicated across multiple randomized controlled trials published in NEJM and JAMA Psychiatry. Single-infusion protocols exist in research settings but are not standard clinical practice for durable response.

The full 6-session course occupies roughly 3 weeks of calendar time, with each appointment consuming approximately half a workday. Patients commuting from Madison, Athens, Decatur, or Florence should factor travel time into their scheduling.

Typical Ketamine Treatment Timeline

  • Week 1–3: 6 infusions (3 per week), each appointment 2–2.5 hours
  • Days 1–3: Some patients notice a response after the first or second infusion
  • Week 4 onward: Response assessed; maintenance schedule determined if applicable
  • Maintenance infusions (if needed): Typically 1 session every 4–8 weeks
  • Chronic pain protocols: Longer individual sessions, different scheduling structure

When Will You Know If It's Working?

This is where ketamine differs most sharply from SSRIs and SNRIs. Antidepressants like sertraline, escitalopram, or venlafaxine require 4–8 weeks before meaningful response. Ketamine may produce measurable antidepressant effects within 24 hours of a single infusion in responsive patients — a mechanism that is distinct from serotonergic drugs. Ketamine acts on NMDA glutamate receptors, not the serotonin system.

In treatment-resistant depression trials, including the Berman et al. study and subsequent NIMH research, approximately 70% of patients who had failed two or more antidepressants showed a meaningful response. Roughly 30% do not respond. The provider you're connected with through the Huntsville ketamine clinic referral network should discuss both figures with you before treatment begins.

⚠️ Important: Rapid improvement in the first 24–48 hours does not mean the underlying condition is resolved. Effects from a single infusion typically last 1–2 weeks without a full course. Stopping after one session because you feel better is a common and consequential mistake. Complete the initial 6-session protocol before evaluating long-term response.

Alabama-Specific Scheduling Considerations

Alabama's private insurance market generally does not cover IV ketamine infusions for psychiatric indications — consistent with the national picture. Esketamine (Spravato), the FDA-approved nasal spray indicated for treatment-resistant depression, has better insurance coverage but a different administration model; it is not interchangeable with IV ketamine. Most Huntsville-area patients pay out of pocket for IV infusions and treat the 3-week initial course as a defined, bounded expense.

Federal employees and contractors at Redstone Arsenal should be aware that TRICARE and federal employee health plans also typically exclude IV ketamine. Confirm your coverage before your first appointment. You can request a consultation to discuss cost and scheduling before committing to a full course. The American Society of Ketamine Physicians also publishes patient preparation guidelines, including a list of questions to ask a provider before your first infusion.

Bottom Line

A single IV ketamine infusion runs 40–60 minutes; your total appointment is 2–2.5 hours. A standard initial course is 6 infusions over 2–3 weeks. Response — when it occurs — may appear within 24 hours of the first or second infusion, not weeks. Approximately 70% of treatment-resistant depression patients respond in published trials; roughly 30% do not. Ketamine is a clinically serious intervention. Plan your schedule accordingly, arrange transportation for every session, and complete the full protocol before evaluating results.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a ketamine infusion take from start to finish?

The IV infusion itself runs 40–60 minutes for psychiatric conditions such as depression and PTSD. Including pre-infusion preparation and post-infusion monitoring, most patients spend 2–2.5 hours at the clinic per appointment. You cannot drive afterward, so arrange a ride home before every session.

How many ketamine sessions do you need for depression?

The standard initial course for treatment-resistant depression is 6 infusions delivered over 2–3 weeks, typically three sessions per week. This schedule is based on the Berman et al. protocol and has been validated in NIMH-funded trials. Some patients require maintenance sessions every 4–8 weeks after the initial course to sustain response.

How quickly does ketamine work for depression?

Unlike SSRIs such as sertraline or escitalopram — which require 4–8 weeks — ketamine produces measurable antidepressant effects within 24 hours in responsive patients. This rapid onset is due to ketamine's action on NMDA glutamate receptors rather than the serotonin system. Response typically becomes clearer after the second or third infusion in a 6-session course.

How long do ketamine infusions take for chronic pain versus depression?

Psychiatric infusions run 40–60 minutes. Chronic pain protocols — especially for CRPS or neuropathic pain — often run 2–4 hours per session at different dosing parameters. The longer duration reflects a higher cumulative dose requirement for pain indications compared to the 0.5 mg/kg standard used in depression trials.

Can I go back to work after a ketamine infusion in Huntsville?

Most patients should not return to cognitively demanding work the same day as an infusion. Dissociative effects resolve within the monitored recovery period, but residual effects on concentration and reaction time are possible. Engineers, analysts, and defense contractors in the Huntsville area typically schedule sessions on days when they can take a half-day rather than returning to detailed technical work.

Does Alabama insurance cover ketamine infusions?

Alabama private insurance plans and federal plans including TRICARE generally do not cover IV ketamine for psychiatric indications. Esketamine (Spravato), the FDA-approved nasal spray, has broader insurance coverage but is a different product with a different administration model. Most patients in the Huntsville area pay out of pocket for IV infusions; confirming your specific plan's coverage before scheduling is strongly recommended.

What happens if ketamine doesn't work?

Approximately 30% of treatment-resistant depression patients do not respond to a full 6-session IV ketamine course. Non-response does not mean all options are exhausted — other interventions including TMS, MAOI antidepressants, lithium augmentation, or clinical trials at UAH or Vanderbilt may be appropriate next steps. A reputable provider will discuss these alternatives honestly rather than recommend indefinite ketamine maintenance without demonstrated benefit.

Is the ketamine infusion timeline different for PTSD than for depression?

The infusion duration and number of sessions are generally similar — 40–60 minutes per infusion, 6 infusions over 2–3 weeks — for both PTSD and treatment-resistant depression. Evidence for ketamine in PTSD has grown substantially, with trials published in JAMA Psychiatry showing significant reduction in PTSD symptom scores compared to midazolam control. Some PTSD protocols incorporate psychotherapy sessions between infusions, which affects overall scheduling.
