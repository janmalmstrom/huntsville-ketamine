What Conditions Qualify for Ketamine Infusion Therapy?

IV ketamine has the strongest clinical evidence for treatment-resistant depression (TRD) — defined as failing to achieve adequate relief after two or more adequate antidepressant trials. Common prior medications include SSRIs such as sertraline or escitalopram, SNRIs such as venlafaxine or duloxetine, and augmentation agents including lithium and lamotrigine.

The clinic also evaluates patients with PTSD, chronic pain conditions including CRPS and fibromyalgia-related neuropathy, and anxiety disorders where conventional treatment has been insufficient. For a full list, see the conditions treated page.

💡 Clinical note: Esketamine (Spravato) is an FDA-approved nasal spray derived from ketamine — it is not the same as IV ketamine. IV ketamine allows more precise dosing control and carries a larger clinical evidence base for treatment-resistant cases. The two are not interchangeable.

What Does the Evidence Say About Outcomes?

According to NIMH-funded research and the landmark Berman et al. trials, IV ketamine produces a clinically meaningful antidepressant response in approximately 70% of patients with treatment-resistant depression — often within 24 hours of the first infusion. The mechanism is NMDA glutamate receptor antagonism, which is pharmacologically distinct from SSRIs and SNRIs.

Roughly 30% of patients do not respond. That figure matters. Ketamine is a serious clinical option — not a guaranteed outcome. The provider network connected through this site uses the Berman et al. protocol (0.5 mg/kg over 40 minutes IV), the dosing standard validated in NIMH-funded trials.

⚠️ Important: If you or someone you know is in crisis, contact the 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline by calling or texting 988. Ketamine therapy requires scheduling and intake — it is not an emergency intervention. Do not delay crisis care to arrange a ketamine consultation.

How many ketamine infusions will I need?

The standard induction protocol is 6 IV infusions delivered over 2–3 weeks, following the Berman et al. dosing standard of 0.5 mg/kg administered over 40 minutes. Some patients require maintenance infusions every 4–8 weeks after the initial series to sustain the antidepressant effect. Your response after sessions 3–4 often predicts whether the full course will be beneficial.

How is IV ketamine different from Spravato (esketamine)?

Spravato is an FDA-approved nasal spray form of esketamine (the S-enantiomer of ketamine), approved specifically for treatment-resistant depression and major depressive disorder with suicidal ideation. IV ketamine allows more precise dose titration, has a longer research history, and is used for a broader range of conditions including PTSD and chronic pain. The two are clinically distinct and not interchangeable.

What are the side effects of IV ketamine infusions?

The most common side effects during infusion are dissociation, mild perceptual distortions, transient increases in blood pressure, and nausea — most resolve within 30–60 minutes of infusion completion. Serious adverse events are rare when administered under physician supervision at evidence-based doses. You will need a designated driver for the remainder of the day and should not operate heavy machinery or make major decisions for 24 hours post-infusion.

Does ketamine work if antidepressants haven't helped me?

Ketamine works through NMDA glutamate receptor antagonism — a completely different pathway than SSRIs, SNRIs, or tricyclic antidepressants, which target monoamine systems. According to NIMH-funded research, IV ketamine produces a clinical response in approximately 70% of patients who have failed two or more adequate antidepressant trials. Approximately 30% of patients do not respond, and a physician consultation before treatment will help assess your likelihood of candidacy.
